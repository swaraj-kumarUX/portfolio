OTA Reconciliation

Operators didn't need a cleaner table of mismatches — they needed to know whether a difference was worth their time. I redesigned the flow around that, tested two directions head-to-head with real operators, and shipped the one that made the "why" visible without a click.

RoleUX lead, solo — discovery through handoff
TeamredBus reconciliation team · Bitla/ticketsimply engineering
ToolsFigma · FigJam · HTML prototyping
TimeframeMay – Sep 2026
StatusDesign-validated, pre-launch
Objective

Design a reconciliation experience that explains why a number is wrong, not just that it is — for operators who don't want to learn a new system, they want to understand their money.

The existing flow, and the tool operators actually reached for instead, both stopped at "here's what doesn't match." Neither ever explained why — so every reconciliation still ended in manual digging.

Context

What operators were dealing with

Bus operators reconcile every invoice redBus sends them against their own records — checking that what they were paid matches what they believe they're owed, PNR by PNR, invoice after invoice. The existing Ticketsimply flow does the mechanical part correctly: it fetches the report, runs the comparison, lists what doesn't match.

What it never does is say why. An operator gets a spreadsheet of flagged rows and has to go dig — manually, invoice by invoice — before deciding whether a difference is even worth chasing.

Scoping

Design challenges & scope

This is a trust product before it's a UI problem — operators act on these numbers with real money. That shaped how I scoped the work.

Challenge

Financial data an operator can act on without re-verifying it by hand — while the tool they use today (Gaadi Kharcha) has trained them not to trust an explanation they can't check.

Scope

Field research with real operators · competitive teardown of Gaadi Kharcha · a redesigned auto-reconciliation flow · a tested summary screen · an AI Summary layer with explicit trust rules · production handoff.

Process

Three decisions that carried the redesign

01

Reframe the question — from "what" to "why"

Two in-person operator interviews turned a screen-level brief into a product-level reframe: operators don't want a cleaner mismatch table, they want to know whether a difference is worth their time.

02

Cut the friction operators never asked to keep

Two manual uploads became an auto-reconciliation flow, syncing directly from redPro via API — removing the exact step operators complained about, unprompted, in research.

03

Earn trust with rules, not just polish

The AI Summary layer only states what it can prove — a rule that exists because I caught it lying to me first, in my own first draft.

01 · Research

Two operators, one afternoon each

I took a printed questionnaire on the road and sat down with two operators in person — Seabird Tourists and Rajesh Transports — expecting to validate a screen. Their answers reframed the whole product instead.

Seabird Tourists · 10 May 2026

"Reconciliation means confirming me the difference and where the difference is going — it's like matching."

Rajesh Transports · 12 May 2026

"Justification." — the entire answer to "what does resolved mean to you."

Neither operator described wanting a cleaner table. Both described wanting to be told why. Two more answers on those sheets fed directly into shipped decisions:

Rajesh, unprompted

"Offers and deals opted by operators are not tracked, and that creates difference."

Shipped as

"Incorrect offer charges" exists as its own discrepancy category, rather than a generic "other."

Rajesh, on default detail level

"Don't want to see every detail — summary, see root-level summary, service and bus number. They want NRR."

Shipped as

The AI Summary's first rule: lead with the state, not the problem count.

Both operators, independently, named Gaadi Kharcha as what they actually use today — Seabird called it "easy to use for any layman." It has the same gap: two manual uploads per run, and mismatches flagged without ever being explained.

"If I know WHY, the difference is manageable."

The line that ended up driving every downstream decision
02 · Redesign

Auto-sync, not two manual uploads

The original flow required uploading both a bank statement and an OTA invoice by hand. I redesigned it into an auto-reconciliation flow: Bitla GDS pulls data directly from redPro, redBus's own operator database, via API — and the bank statement upload becomes optional rather than blocking.

Non-redBus OTAs (AbhiBus, Paytm, and others) still require manual upload, since no equivalent API exists for them yet. Whether that stays the final state or is a phase-1 limitation is an open question I flagged rather than resolved unilaterally.

Testing

Two real layouts, tested head-to-head

Rather than ship a single direction on instinct, I designed two competing versions of the core summary screen and ran a structured comparison test — comprehension questions with known correct answers, timed tasks, and one question that cut past politeness: "Would you send a dispute to redBus based only on this, without checking by hand?" A screen operators like but don't trust enough to act on hasn't solved the actual problem.

✓ Selected
Concept 1 — V1

Chart breakup + accordions

Two donut charts visualize the invoice and difference-receivable breakup by category, on top of the same accordion detail as V2. Operators saw the biggest slice of the gap before opening a single row.

Concept 2 — V2

Accordions only

Simpler, cleaner-looking screen — same headline number, same accordion detail underneath, but no visual breakup. Operators could see that there was a gap, not what kind, without digging in.

Why V1 won

It wasn't information architecture — both versions gave operators identical accordions and identical PNR-level detail. It was data visualization: V1's charts made the "why" visible without a click. V2 made operators go looking for it, which quietly reintroduced the exact problem the redesign existed to fix.

03 · System

The AI Summary, and the rule that governs it

On top of the detailed tables, the AI Summary answers the operator's real question — fine or not fine, and what kind of not-fine — in one read, before they touch a table.

01

Lead with the state

"97% reconciled," not "33 mismatches found." Opening with the problem count turns every invoice into a perceived crisis.

02

Never overclaim confidence

Caught a real bug where the category breakdown didn't sum to the headline number. Now it only states what it can trace to a real value.

03

Traceable, not just readable

Every claim is one click from its source — clicking "33 PNRs" filters straight to those rows.

04

Scoped to where it helps

The escalation screen gets a terse one-line version, so it never competes with the audit trail for attention.

Honesty

What I didn't build

Real scope, stated plainly rather than smoothed over:

Placeholder accordions

"Escalated PNRs" and "Invoice Deductions" show correct counts with only one category fully wired to real row data.

SLA-nudge, unresolved

What happens when an OTA doesn't respond in time was left explicitly open rather than guessed at.

IA feedback, flagged not fixed

Seabird's note that the app "has too many tabs" is an app-level signal above what one module can fix alone.

Pre-launch

Validated against real operator comprehension and behavior before a line of production code — not yet measured live.

Visual language

Built in BITLA's real design tokens

No exploratory reskin — every screen was built in the actual production BITLA system, so what operators tested is what could ship as-is.
